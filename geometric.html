A Geometric Intuition for Power Series

My first encounter with power series confused me because they look like polynomials (∑anxn) but N goes to infinity. Usually with polynomials you can have an idea of its shape based on the degree but when N goes to infinity you’re reluctant to imagine a shape because you don’t have a definitive leading term. It’s worse if you’re coming from a lecture in sequences and series of functions because you would have just seen a series of functions defined as ∑fn(x). Because f1(x),… are arbitrary it can be intimidating to imagine what it means to sum them in the first place even for a finite sum and since power series are infinite sums that nervousness can transfer.

But from the definition of a power series as ∑anxn, if you think of n = 1 and n = 2 you get f1(x) = a1x, and f2(x) = a2x2 and their sum is actually just a quadratic. Now add the next term n = 3 and that sum becomes a cubic. Add another and it’s a quartic.

Partial sums of 1/(1−x) at degrees 1 through 4. Dashed line: the target function. Orange lines: radius of convergence.

Adding subsequent terms results in a polynomial which is the partial sum of the power series. Each partial sum corresponds to either a bowl-shape when the degree is even or an S-shape when the degree is odd. This alternation leads to the question: is there ever a region where adding an extra term eventually leaves the value of the series undisturbed?

When such a region exists, it’s called the radius of convergence.
