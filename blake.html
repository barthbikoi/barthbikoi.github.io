The Rose’s Response to Blake and the Worm

After William Blake’s “The Sick Rose”
By Barth Bikoi

Blake, you have spoken well, and indeed,
this worm hath found out me;
yet I ask: how may it be so,
since I suppose it hath not light to guide it in the storm?
Shall I suppose it sees even in the dark?
And if it does, why could I not see it coming?
Or did I ignore the portents,
the stories from unfortunate roses of the past?
Or, if the night obscures the worm’s vision,
shall my bright petals be to blame,
since they might have inadvertently exposed me?
Or does my nectar appeal to the worm?
Did you, O worm, destroy anything else?
And you, Blake, why lament ye for me?
Shall ye but tend to me when your writing is done?
Who shall rescue me from the worm
that sees me, but that I see not,
or that, if I see, I would fain welcome?
And welcome my own destruction with?

Wherefore in ultimate desolation I exclaim: Alas for me!
