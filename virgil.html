Duty That Mourns: Virgil, Augustine, and the Weight of Necessary Violence

an essay

“Ah but then, when the son of Anchises saw his dying look, his face — that face so ashen, awesome in death — he groaned from his depths in pity, reached out his hand as this picture of love for a father pierced his heart, and said: ‘Forlorn young soldier, what can Aeneas, in all honor, give you to match your glory now? What gifts are worthy of such a noble spirit? Keep your armor that gave you so much joy. I give you back to your fathers’ ash and shades if it offers any solace. And this, at least, may comfort you for a death so cruel, unlucky boy: you went down under the hand of great Aeneas.’”

— Virgil, Aeneid, Book X [970–982]
